# Newfoundland Wafer Company

**Status:** proposed business; concept plan v0.1, 21 October 2026. No company, mineral rights, customers, facilities, or financing are represented as secured. All proposed budgets below are original screening assumptions in **constant 2026 Canadian dollars**, not supplier quotations or investment valuations.

## Purpose and first business

Build a Newfoundland supplier of traceable silicon wafers and substrate services, with a long-term option to integrate qualified local mineral feedstock. The proposed first business is importing qualified wafers, independently checking selected properties through contracted laboratories, supplying small lots, and coordinating outsourced reclaim or finishing. This lets the team learn customer specifications and earn revenue before owning a mine, chemical refinery, or crystal-growth plant.

The eventual product is a circular, polished **silicon** wafer; silicone is a different family of materials. Boron-doped p-type silicon is one useful specification, not a requirement for every customer. Select diameter, crystal orientation, resistivity, thickness, surface finish, contamination limits, and defect criteria with named prospective buyers before selecting machinery. SUMCO describes crystal growth from purified polysilicon with controlled doping, followed by slicing, lapping, etching, polishing, cleaning, and inspection. [W02](https://www.sumcosi.com/english/products/process/)

## What the Grey River idea establishes—and what it still needs

A Newfoundland government document describes Grey River tungsten mineralization in quartz-bearing veins. It supports the presence of quartz in that geological setting. It does **not** establish a mineable high-purity quartz reserve, semiconductor-grade feedstock, current access rights, or economic recovery for this proposal. Treat Grey River as a **candidate to investigate**, with qualified geology and independent laboratory work; the business must remain viable using other qualified sources. [W01](https://www.gov.nl.ca/em/files/mines-investments-companysheets-tungsten.pdf)

The first resource study should answer these questions:

1. Which exact parcel and geological unit are proposed, who holds the relevant rights, and what permissions and community relationships are needed to investigate it?
2. Do representative samples establish consistent mineralogy, trace impurities, inclusions, and variability across the candidate body? A clean-looking hand specimen is insufficient evidence.
3. What saleable product is achievable after processing, with what recovery, waste stream, energy demand, and delivered cost?
4. Will a real buyer qualify that product? Silicon-smelter feedstock and quartz for semiconductor crucibles require separate qualification.
5. Does an independently reviewed resource and project study justify further spending against the alternative of buying feedstock?

Do not choose a refinery or wafer-factory site merely because a candidate mine is nearby. Compare power reliability and connection costs, water and effluent treatment, transport, workforce access, maintenance response, and community preferences independently.

## Technical scope

The full industrial chain has distinct businesses and skills:

| Step | Material or output | Proposed approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resource and beneficiation | Qualified quartz or silica feedstock | Commission exploration and test work; use partners before owning extraction |
| Silicon smelting | Metallurgical-grade silicon | Initially buy; consider a separate joint venture only after a competitive energy and feedstock case |
| Chemical purification | Electronic-grade polysilicon | Buy from a qualified producer; later consider licensed or partnered production |
| Crystal growth | Controlled-doping single-crystal silicon ingot | Develop after finishing has demonstrated a customer market |
| Wafer forming | Sliced, finished, polished wafers | Begin with outsourced services; install a tightly scoped line after qualification |
| Inspection and release | Measured, traceable, accepted lots | Build quality ownership from the first sale |

WACKER identifies quartz reduction as an upstream route to silicon and distinguishes its semiconductor polysilicon business. SUMCO documents the downstream crystal-growth and wafer-forming steps. This table is a proposed ownership sequence, not a chemical process recipe. [W03](https://reports.wacker.com/2021/annual-report/sustainable-solutions/ultrapure-silicon-for-solar-power.html) [W02](https://www.sumcosi.com/english/products/process/)

**Two quartz opportunities must stay separate.** Quartz can be raw material for silicon production; exceptionally pure quartz can also become the crucible that holds molten silicon during crystal growth. A quartz crucible remains silicon dioxide, while the wafer is crystalline silicon. The Quartz Corp describes the crucible application. Passing one customer's specification would not establish suitability for the other product. [W04](https://www.thequartzcorp.com/silicon-wafer-production)

## Customers, offering, and revenue

**Initial customer hypotheses:** university and industrial research laboratories, small MEMS or sensor developers, equipment manufacturers needing test substrates, and specialty fabrication lines. These are segments for discovery, not verified demand. Sell beyond Newfoundland from the start; the other three proposed companies do not yet constitute a market.

**Proposed differentiator:** documented provenance, dependable small-lot availability, clear specifications, responsive quality investigations, and access to characterization or reclaim services. Buyers need a reason to switch from established suppliers. Local branding alone does not establish value.

Revenue can come from wafer resale margins, contracted inspection coordination, custom lot preparation through qualified partners, and later finishing or reclaim charges. Keep research/test wafers separately identified from production-qualified prime wafers. Reclaimed wafers must not be represented as interchangeable with prime device substrates.

Before buying production tools, conduct 20 structured buyer interviews, obtain five written specification-and-volume statements, complete three paid pilot orders, and establish at least two repeat customers. These are proposed management gates, not a forecast of adoption. Validate return rights, credit terms, contamination responsibility, delivery expectations, and whether buyers accept the proposed laboratories and suppliers.

## Capital, time, and scope

**Timing runs from funded kickoff with a competent team.** Funding delays, site selection, permits, equipment delivery, and customer qualification can extend it. First revenue is different from commercial qualification or profitability. These are alternative or expanding investment scopes; do not add the rows as a project total. Later-stage financing must credit reusable earlier assets and separately price integration.

| Stage | Proposed activity | Total financing envelope | Indicative allocation within envelope | Time to stage outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| W0 | Customer, supplier, site, and Grey River screening | C$0.1–0.3M | Professional work and sampling design; included within W1 if pursued together | 3–6 months; no manufacturing promised |
| W1 | Technical distribution and qualified outsourced services | **C$2–5M** | C$0.4–1.0M equipment/fit-out; C$1.0–2.4M team, lab services and overhead; C$0.3–0.7M inventory/receivables; C$0.3–0.9M contingency | First paid shipment **6–18 months**; 24-month initial runway target |
| W2 | Focused 100/150 mm finishing or reclaim pilot using imported material | **C$15–50M** | Approximately C$12–40M installed plant, utilities and contingency; C$3–10M qualification and operating runway | **2–5 years** to a qualified pilot product from funded stage start |
| W3 | Specialty crystal growth plus prime-wafer production | **C$100–500M** | Approximately C$80–400M installed plant and contingency; C$20–100M qualification, inventory and ramp losses | **5–10 years** from integrated programme kickoff; customer/product dependent |
| W4 | Mine, smelting, electronic-grade purification, and wafer integration through partners/JVs | **C$1–5B+** | Illustratively C$0.8–4B+ plants/utilities/contingency; C$0.2–1B+ commissioning, qualification and working capital | **10–20+ years** from programme kickoff, conditional on each gate |

These envelopes are deliberately broad, low-confidence estimates. W1 financing includes pre-revenue operating burn, inventory/receivables working capital, and contingency; no sales receipts are deducted from the initial financing need. W1 assumes a small leased operation with contracted advanced analysis, not an owned semiconductor production cleanroom. W2 assumes a limited pilot product set, not high-volume 300 mm prime wafers. W3 assumes specialty capacity and an experienced technical partner. W4 is not a commitment that C$5B would be sufficient for all conceivable capacities or locations. Capacity, yields, technology licences, power connections and effluent infrastructure remain uncosted design variables that could move the estimate materially.

**Scale checks, not direct cost estimates:** NIST lists approximately **US$4B** of expected capital expenditure across the GlobalWafers Texas and Missouri programme. WACKER reports a semiconductor-polysilicon production line at an existing site costing **over €300M**, commissioned in July 2025. These examples explain why a complete wafer supply chain belongs in a different capital category from a distribution startup. They are retained in their original currencies and scopes; neither is a Newfoundland quotation. [W05](https://www.nist.gov/chips/globalwafers-texas-sherman) [W06](https://reports.wacker.com/2025/annual-report/for-our-shareholders/highlights-from-2025.html)

## Team and financing strategy

For W1, plan for four to eight core people by the end of the first funded period: a commercial lead, a wafer/process specialist, a quality and supplier specialist, an applications or metrology engineer, and operations staff as orders require. Contract specialist geological, environmental, legal and finance work. This headcount is an assumption underlying the W1 payroll envelope.

For W2 onward, add experienced process integration, facilities, maintenance, automation, environmental health and safety, and shift operations leadership. Confirm staffing against the actual line and shift schedule before financing. A mine, smelter, chemical purification plant, and wafer facility require separate accountable operating teams even if held under one company.

Fund W0/W1 using founder/seed capital and paid customer work; count grants only once an award is confirmed. Finance W2 only after technical acceptance and demand evidence. Treat W3/W4 as strategic-partner and project-finance programmes requiring qualified products, binding supply arrangements, credible operating leadership, and downside cases. No subsidy or partner commitment is assumed.

## Unit economics to replace with quotations

Use contribution per accepted wafer, not merely the purchase-to-sale price spread:

`contribution = net selling price − landed substrate cost − variable inspection/processing − variable packing/freight − expected scrap/returns`

**Illustrative arithmetic only:** a C$200 net sale less C$125 landed substrate, C$35 variable inspection/fulfilment, and C$10 expected scrap/returns leaves **C$30 contribution**. At C$1.2M annual fixed cash operating cost, operating break-even would require **40,000 accepted wafers per year** at that mix. This excludes financing, taxes, depreciation and replacement capital. It is not a market price, volume forecast, or profitability claim. If contribution falls to C$15, required volume doubles. This example makes clear that a low-margin resale business may fail even when technical delivery succeeds.

For manufacturing, add stage-by-stage yield, tool utilization, wafer diameter, accepted specification, water, electricity, consumables, preventive maintenance, depreciation, and quality losses. Compare cost per customer-accepted wafer against delivered imports. Do not justify a plant using revenue from a sister company that has no funded orders.

## Decision gates and stopping rules

| Gate | Evidence required to proceed | If evidence fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demand | Paid pilots, repeat orders, achievable contribution margin, documented specifications | Narrow the offering or remain a small service business |
| Material | Independent representative assays; repeatable processing recovery; qualified buyer acceptance | Use other feedstock; stop the Grey River branch |
| Finishing | Consecutive conforming lots, documented measurement capability, acceptable returns and yield | Retain outsourced finishing; do not scale the line |
| Factory | Quoted equipment and utility scope, site/permitting pathway, experienced team, funded qualification runway | Defer construction and retain a partner model |
| Integration | Resource study, customer commitments, licensed know-how, competitive landed economics in downside cases | Keep upstream stages external |

Commission process and environmental reviews covering silica dust, high-temperature operations, chemical handling, wastewater, waste management, emergency response and closure. These are required planning workstreams, not operating instructions. The provincial environmental-assessment guide identifies designated undertakings and project-dependent assessment; establish the requirements for the actual proposal with the relevant authorities before physical work. [W07](https://www.gov.nl.ca/eccc/files/GUIDE-TO-THE-PROCESS_Aug-2025.pdf)

## First 90 days and first year

**Days 1–30:** recruit a business-development lead and wafer-industry technical adviser; define two prospective product specifications; build the customer interview list; identify suppliers and independent laboratories; commission a desk-based Grey River rights/geology review.

**Days 31–60:** complete interviews; obtain supplier and laboratory budget quotations; compare two candidate operating locations; draft the lot-traceability and acceptance specification; design any field sampling with qualified professionals and the required permissions.

**Days 61–90:** publish the evidence register, W1 cash model and a decision memo. Advance only if buyer interest is tied to measurable specifications, procurement is feasible, and the financing covers the qualification period. Keep confidential customer material out of the public repository.

**Months 4–12:** secure paid pilot orders, operate the quality process, record rejected and accepted lots, test delivery economics, and decide whether W2 deserves a feasibility study. Publish de-identified lessons and revisions. A successful year-one outcome is a defensible product and customer case, not an unsupported announcement of a mine-to-wafer factory.

## How the company contributes to the four-company ecosystem

Supply Newfoundland Foundry only after its substrate specification is defined and the material is qualified. Supply test wafers to Newfoundland Nanomachines where appropriate. Provide substrate data to Newfoundland Chip Company through the foundry's process design kit and design rules; chip designers do not bypass foundry process qualification by selecting arbitrary wafers.

Publish original business plans, interface specifications, and non-confidential qualification methods under the repository's MIT licence. Third-party specifications, proprietary process recipes, customer records and supplier IP remain subject to their own rights. Commercial implementation is encouraged without promising that all third-party technology rights are cleared.
