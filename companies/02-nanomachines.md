# Newfoundland Nanomachines

**Status:** proposed company; concept plan dated 2026-10-21. No company incorporation, customers, partnerships, equipment ownership or financing is asserted.

**Mission:** build a Newfoundland business that maintains, automates and eventually manufactures selected semiconductor and thin-film production equipment. Earn external revenue while developing the engineers and service capability that a future Newfoundland foundry will need.

**Recommended launch:** C$2–5 million for an 18–24-month service-and-controls business, with first paid work targeted 6–12 months after funding. A production lithography stepper is a later, separately financed research and partnership decision.

## 1. What the company sells

The first offer is equipment uptime and traceable process data: vacuum-system diagnostics, authorized refurbishment, controls retrofits, maintenance planning, spare-parts support, and documented commissioning. Target research laboratories, thin-film manufacturers and specialist industrial customers across Canada and other reachable markets. These are customer hypotheses to test; Newfoundland demand alone is not an established business case.

The first original product should be one narrow, serviceable R&D sputter-deposition platform, or a repeatable subsystem sold into existing platforms. Buy qualified pumps, gauges, power supplies and other subsystems; do local systems engineering, controls, mechanical integration, acceptance testing and support. Revenue comes from engineering fees, equipment sales, installation and recurring support contracts. Being open with original plans can support adoption while the business charges for reliable execution.

Incumbents already offer integrated sputtering, evaporation and automation products: Kurt J. Lesker's PVD 75 is one reference product, not an available design to copy or a price quotation. Its existence is evidence of an established product category and competition. [E01 — manufacturer product page](https://www.lesker.com/process-equipment-division/thin-film-systems/pro-line-pvd-75-deposition-platform.cfm)

## 2. Separate the equipment categories

| Category | Role | Proposed scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vacuum service, controls and data systems | Keep equipment operating and record process history | First revenue; support only platforms for which documentation, parts, access and competence are secured |
| R&D sputtering / physical vapour deposition | Deposit thin films; customers qualify each material and application | First original equipment candidate after paid service work identifies a repeatable need |
| Production deposition equipment | Meet customer throughput, contamination, reliability and process-control requirements | Later platform, independently qualified by paying industrial customers |
| Contact/proximity mask aligner | Transfer a mask pattern without a reduction projection lens | Possible research, MEMS or packaging niche; its capability does not establish CPU or DRAM process capability |
| Projection stepper | Project a reduced pattern onto successive wafer fields with precise alignment | Partner-led long-term option; precision optics, stages, metrology and process integration are separate major challenges |
| Advanced DUV/EUV scanner | High-performance exposure equipment within an advanced manufacturing flow | Outside the funded base case; procure from qualified suppliers if a future fab needs it |

SUSS identifies proximity alignment applications including packaging and MEMS. Nikon separately specifies i-line reduction steppers: its NSR-SF155 lists 280 nm resolution and 25 nm single-machine overlay. Those specifications are different measurements, and neither should be equated to a marketing process-node name. [E02 — SUSS mask aligner](https://www.suss.com/en/products-solutions/imaging-solutions/mask-alignment/ma300-gen3); [E03 — Nikon equipment specifications](https://www.nikon.com/business/semi/lineup/)

Buying a used stepper and building a competitive stepper business are different investments. ASML offers refurbished PAS 5500 and early TWINSCAN systems backed by its refurbishment process and support; this is a procurement alternative to investigate, not a promise that equipment is available or economical for Newfoundland. [E04 — ASML refurbished systems](https://www.asml.com/en/products/refurbished-systems)

## 3. Capital, timing and decision gates

All amounts below are **original concept-level planning assumptions in constant 2026 Canadian dollars**, not vendor quotes or financing offers. Each row is the gross funding envelope for the specified stage, including its stated operating runway and contingency. A follow-on stage replaces a new period of planning; rows are not a total-company valuation or a cumulative fundraising forecast. Deduct usable existing assets, operating cash generation and overlapping work only in a dated financing model. Do not count unapproved grants as cash.

| Stage | Capital envelope | Time from that stage's funding | Funded outcome and release gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Service, retrofit and applications business | C$2–5m | 18–24 months; first paid service target 6–12 months | 5–8 staff hired in sequence; workshop and diagnostic capability; at least three unrelated paying customers; measured delivery margin |
| One original R&D deposition platform | C$8–25m | 3–5 years | Design, prototype units, application validation, production documentation, warranty reserve and pilot deliveries; proceed only with multiple paid design partners |
| Qualified specialty production platform | C$30–100m+ | 5–10 years | Supplier qualification, manufacturing scale-up and field-service coverage for one defined production niche; at least two independent customer qualifications |
| Lithography partnership feasibility | C$2–10m, study and demonstrators only | 2–3 years, after a partner-led proposal | Independent optics/stage/metrology review, market need, IP pathway, funded partner commitments and a credible full development estimate |

**The study budget does not buy a commercial stepper.** A production-stepper program cannot be responsibly priced here without a target specification, experienced partners and a development plan. Its potential funding scale is hundreds of millions to billions of dollars, with low confidence and a multi-decade commercialization risk. Do not insert that unscoped option into the base-case total. EUV replication is not a funded milestone. For scale, ASML reported €4.7 billion of annual R&D across its portfolio and 5,100 suppliers for 2025; these are incumbent-company figures, not an estimate for this proposal. [E05 — ASML 2025 annual report](https://investor.asml.com/annual-reports/2025)

Owning precision optics production, a large cleanroom or an international 24/7 support network requires another plan. The startup envelope includes customer discovery; do not add a second discovery budget for the same work.

## 4. Customer and economic model

Start with a sales lead and an experienced vacuum/process engineer. Interview at least 20 prospective customers outside the proposed four-company group. Obtain five detailed accounts of downtime, unsupported equipment or data problems; seek three paid diagnostic or retrofit engagements. No interview, letter of interest or related-company purchase should be reported as a booked external order.

Price service work from fully loaded delivery cost, travel, parts and a defined acceptance test. Offer fixed-scope diagnostics before open-ended repairs. A support agreement should specify covered equipment, spare-parts ownership, response time, exclusions and geography. Promise round-the-clock response only after staffing it; incumbent ASML's service description illustrates why support is a substantial operational function. [E06 — ASML customer support](https://www.asml.com/en/products/customer-support)

**Illustrative arithmetic, not a revenue forecast:** an R&D tool sold for C$500,000 with C$300,000 of components, assembly and direct labour, plus C$50,000 for installation and expected warranty work, contributes C$150,000 before fixed engineering, sales and administration. At C$3m annual fixed cost, 20 such sales cover that fixed cost before financing costs and tax. If contribution falls to C$100,000, the requirement becomes 30 sales. This is why a handful of prototypes does not establish a sustainable product business.

For service, model billable engineer-days × realized rate, subtract direct labour, travel, parts and rework, then subtract remaining overhead. Use the same cost once: personnel included in direct delivery costs must not also be included in fixed-cost calculations. Track deposits, milestone receipts and acceptance delays separately from accounting revenue.

## 5. Team and Newfoundland location

The initial team needs commercial leadership, senior vacuum/process engineering, electrical and controls engineering, mechanical integration, and field service. A few people may cover multiple roles at first. Add applications science, quality and supply-chain specialists before promising repeatable equipment performance. Recruit people who have commissioned and supported operating tools, and build apprenticeships around their work.

Memorial's published Physics equipment inventory lists sputter deposition and vacuum evaporation, but the page's stated last update is September 2021. Its Technical Services page lists machining and vacuum-pump repair. These are leads for capability and access discussions; current condition, availability, commercial terms and staffing must be verified. Neither page establishes a partnership or a production semiconductor facility. [E07 — Memorial equipment inventory](https://www.mun.ca/physics/research-and-teaching/research/condensed-matter-equipment/); [E08 — Memorial Mechanical Division](https://www.mun.ca/research/who-we-are/technical-services/mechanical-division/)

Choose premises after checking freight routes, power quality, competent service labour, safe material handling and access to external calibration and analysis. Use a mainland service partner or scheduled travel where customer response requirements exceed what the initial Newfoundland team can support.

## 6. Qualification and interfaces

For each product, publish a customer-approved acceptance specification covering the supported wafer/substrate size, material restrictions, repeatability, contamination limits, uptime measurement and maintenance procedures. Numerical process specifications must come from the actual application and validation work. A test coating or successful laboratory run is not production qualification.

Maintain serialized bills of materials, component provenance, calibration status, software versions, change control, failure reports, spare-parts lifecycle and warranty reserves. Separate clean assembly from repair of contaminated customer equipment. Price professional electrical, machinery and environmental review into development; obtain the certifications and permits required by the eventual installation market. Keep process and hazard engineering in controlled professional design work rather than treating this public plan as operating instructions.

The Newfoundland Foundry is a potential application partner, not a guaranteed customer or the only route to revenue. Require an arm's-length quotation and independent acceptance for any related-company transaction. The foundry must remain able to buy qualified foreign equipment whenever local tools fail its requirements.

## 7. First 90 days and first year

| Period | Work | Evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–30 | Appoint technical/commercial leads; define one service offer; interview ten potential buyers | Named buyer roles, documented problems, candidate platforms and required support geography |
| Days 31–60 | Complete another ten interviews; price equipment, insurance and premises; draft acceptance and service terms | Three supplier quotations where practical; dated budget; explicit gaps in platform authorization and support |
| Days 61–90 | Seek paid pilot scopes; select a limited demonstration setup; compare importing an existing tool with developing one | Three actionable paid proposals, at least one funded pilot decision, and a documented go/no-go review |
| Months 4–6 | Deliver initial diagnostics/retrofits under experienced supervision | Customer acceptance, time/cost records and failure/rework log |
| Months 7–12 | Obtain repeat orders; select a product only if needs repeat across buyers | At least three independent paying customers and a product requirements document supported by their evidence |

Stop or narrow the offer if the founders cannot secure the technical lead, buyers will not pay for diagnosis, required vendor rights are unavailable, or travel and warranty costs erase the delivery margin. Do not purchase a speculative catalogue of nanotechnology equipment to create the appearance of a factory.

## 8. Success by 2056

A defensible outcome is a Newfoundland employer exporting selected qualified tools and services, maintaining a local equipment engineering workforce, and supporting domestic foundry operations. Building a marketable stepper remains an optional achievement contingent on evidence and partners. It must not become a prerequisite that prevents the other companies from purchasing equipment and shipping products.
