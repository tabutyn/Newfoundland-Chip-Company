# Newfoundland Foundry

**Status:** proposed company; open concept plan, no facility, customer commitment or financing assumed.  
**Planning basis:** 21 October 2026; original estimates are constant 2026 Canadian dollars.  
**Mission:** create dependable local semiconductor manufacturing capability, starting with services that customers can buy and expanding only when a qualified process and sufficient demand justify the next investment.

## 1. What this company does

A foundry manufactures devices using a defined, qualified process. A cleanroom and a collection of machines are only part of that business. The company also needs process integration, contamination control, metrology, maintenance, device models, a PDK, yield engineering, production scheduling, traceability, test, environmental systems and customer qualification.

The recommended entry is a **test, metrology and assembly-support business**, followed by selected packaging capabilities and a shared pilot facility. This first stage is a business-development path toward a foundry; it is not represented as wafer fabrication. Package assembly, board assembly and front-end wafer fabrication must be reported separately.

Potential early buyers are university and industrial research teams, Canadian fabless designers and specialized instrument manufacturers needing small-volume testing, package development, failure analysis or reliable manufacturing support. They are customer hypotheses. Ask what service they cannot already obtain on acceptable terms, then sell that service before buying equipment to provide it at larger scale.

CMC already advertises fabrication and packaging access through its network. Investigate partnership and outsourcing before duplicating capacity. Access terms, availability and willingness to partner remain unverified. [C01: CMC MPW and packaging services](https://www.cmc.ca/fab/)

## 2. Scope each stage separately

| Stage | Original gross cash envelope | Time from authorization | What it can credibly deliver |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Test/metrology/assembly-support startup | **C$5–15M** | 24 months of funded startup; first paid services targeted at 12–24 months | A leased, fitted laboratory, trained staff, selected instruments, outside packaging/assembly capacity and customer acceptance processes |
| Limited research/pilot microfabrication | **C$50–200M** | 4–8 years | A specified 100/150mm research or pilot line for a narrow process family; e.g. thin films, test structures or MEMS. Full CMOS capability requires its own supported process-transfer case |
| Specialty commercial wafer fab | **C$1–3B** | 6–10 years to qualified initial production | A prospective 150/200mm mature-process facility, initially screening approximately 3,000–10,000 wafer starts/month; process, scale and price must be validated |
| Advanced DRAM manufacturing partnership | **C$15–40B+ screening allowance only** | 8–15+ years from a viable partnership and funding decision | A separate major industrial program with licensed technology, proven process leadership, large customers and continuing reinvestment |

All ranges are low-confidence planning assumptions to be replaced by site engineering and supplier quotations. Each later envelope describes the **whole named stage**, not a promise that all prior equipment is reusable. Before adding stages, deduct reusable assets and identify new spending explicitly. A packaging laboratory, a MEMS pilot and a commercial CMOS fab are different investments. The DRAM row is excluded from the baseline and may be unaffordable or commercially unattractive even in 2056.

A small mature-process CMOS line could eventually fabricate a small SRAM device or controller. Its die size, performance and economics would differ from a high-density commodity DRAM product. Modern DRAM uses specialized memory structures; access to general CMOS machinery does not establish a qualified DRAM process. Micron's technical overview identifies the capacitor/transistor DRAM cell. [C04: Micron DDR5 overview](https://www.micron.com/products/memory/dram-components/ddr5-sdram)

Calendar ambition does not shorten commissioning. A stage authorized in 2040 with an eight-year qualification path targets 2048, even if an earlier roadmap had shown 2040 production. The stated time includes engineering, delivery, installation, process development and customer qualification; first construction and first qualified product are separate milestones.

The low case buys a narrow selection of tools and outsources most packaging and specialist analysis. The high case can support more in-house capability and a larger team. Neither purchases a complete semiconductor wafer fab, high-volume assembly line or HBM packaging plant. No assumed subsidy or prospective customer revenue reduces these sums.

The low financing case starts with roughly 2–3 core people and uses late hires and outside specialists; the upper case could ramp from 4–6 toward 8–15: business/operations lead, experienced process or packaging engineer, test/metrology lead, technicians, quality and facilities/safety support. Low-case payroll assumes later hiring and outside specialists. A commercial wafer fab later requires a much larger team across shifts; that staffing plan cannot be inferred from laboratory staffing.

## 5. Sell qualified output, not the existence of a facility

Early revenue can come from engineering setup fees, paid test/measurement work, package-development projects and recurring qualified services. Later foundry contracts can combine process-development fees, reserved capacity and wafer sales. Quote turnaround time, acceptance tests, data deliverables, change notification, liability and rework terms. A credible prospective customer must agree that the proposed process is useful and competitively priced.

**Illustrative laboratory sensitivity, assumptions only:** three instruments each producing 1,500 billable hours/year at C$250/hour generate C$1.125M annual revenue. If staff, facilities and other annual cash costs total C$2M, this business loses cash before growth expenditure. Tool ownership does not establish a viable business. The company would need more profitable service mix, higher paid utilization, lower cost or explicit research-facility funding. Avoid double-counting tool hours where a job occupies multiple instruments simultaneously.

For a wafer fab, model the full production path:

`accepted wafers = installed starts capacity × utilization × line yield`

`tested good dies = accepted wafers × gross dies per wafer × die yield × packaging/test yield`

Keep wafer line yield, electrical die yield and packaging/test yield separate. Quote actual die area and edge losses before estimating gross dies per wafer. Utilization is productive demand after maintenance and scheduling constraints, not the fraction of installed equipment switched on.

**Illustrative utilization sensitivity, assumptions only:** at 5,000 starts/month, 60% utilization and 90% line yield, annual accepted wafer output is 32,400. If annual fixed cash costs were C$60M, fixed cost alone would be about C$1,852 per accepted wafer. At 85% utilization and the same line yield, output is 45,900 and fixed cost falls to about C$1,307. Materials, variable utilities, test, scrap, capital recovery, interest and taxes are still additional. These invented inputs demonstrate why contracted demand and stable yield matter; they are not a price or profit forecast for a selected process.

## 6. A process and PDK are independent deliverables

Before financing a commercial CMOS line, specify wafer diameter, product family, device characteristics, thermal/process limits, layer stack, design rules, models, standard cells, memory macros, test structures, reliability requirements and statistical process controls. A node label alone is insufficient.

The first process should usually come through a commercial technology-transfer partner or a tightly scoped research program led by experienced integration engineers. Require the partner to define training, tooling equivalence, contamination limits, intellectual-property rights, qualified suppliers, acceptance tests and continuing support. VSMC's announced use of transferred TSMC technology illustrates that a new foundry can rely on a process partner rather than invent its entire process. [F02](https://www.nxp.com/company/about-nxp/newsroom/NW-VSMC)

Do not assume an open PDK can be installed as a manufacturing recipe. It describes design access to a particular process; the public SKY130 documentation explicitly distinguishes its experimental release from production readiness. [C03](https://skywater-pdk.readthedocs.io/en/main/)

## 7. Stage gates and stop conditions

| Gate | Minimum evidence to commission | Stop or defer if |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Test-services startup | Two unrelated paying customer paths; qualified technical lead; tool quotes; site fit-out plan; affordable outsourcing | Demand is solely a promise from the proposed Chip Company |
| Pilot facility | Defined process family; operating sponsor; external demand; access agreement with research/industrial partners; funded maintenance and staffing | Funding covers construction but not years of operation |
| Commercial fab feasibility | Experienced operator and process-transfer partner; site/utility studies; independently reviewed cost; financing model under downside demand/yield | Only the equipment purchase list is priced |
| Final investment decision | Sufficient binding customer commitments for the financing model; permits; community and rights-holder engagement; executable funding; installation and ramp schedule | The plan needs unspecified future grants or assumes continuous full utilization |
| Production release | Qualified process and PDK; stable yield evidence across lots; customer acceptance; trained shifts; spares and emergency response; traceable quality system | One functioning test chip is being used to claim production readiness |
| DRAM decision | Memory-process/IP partner, credible bit-cost model, large anchor customers and capital for continuing upgrades | A fixed 30-year deadline is the main rationale |

After each gate, publish an evidence summary and revised budget. Reusing a smaller research facility as a teaching/test service is a valid outcome if the commercial fab case fails.

## 8. Site, infrastructure and people

Compare sites on power quality and redundancy, water availability and treatment, wastewater acceptance, appropriate zoning, transport links, vibration, flood/climate exposure, trained workforce, housing, construction capability and expansion options. Mine proximity does not determine fab location. Do not claim cheap electricity or adequate water from a provincial average or a map; obtain site-specific engineering and commercial terms.

Facility design must professionally address chemical/gas handling, exhaust and abatement, emergency systems, occupational safety, waste management and environmental authorization. This repository does not contain operating recipes. Detailed obligations and permits require site-specific review and current government guidance. Engage affected communities and Indigenous rights holders early, before choosing sites or announcing benefits.

Training should combine experienced external hires, local technical education, paid apprenticeships and placements at operating facilities. Universities and colleges are prospective partners; no access, endorsement or participation is assumed.

## 9. Relations with the other three companies

Buy wafers from Newfoundland Wafer Company only when each lot meets the process specification; retain qualified external sources. Buy equipment from Newfoundland Nanomachines when reliability, contamination and process results meet the same standard as alternative suppliers. Offer process access to Newfoundland Chip Company and external designers through documented contracts and a supported PDK.

Keep the foundry commercially independent: publish a policy for customer confidentiality, equal access, process-change control and disputes. Local ownership does not justify preferential technical acceptance or exposure of one customer's design to another. Use outside packaging/test and board manufacturers until the local capability is qualified, with clear responsibility for final product acceptance.

## 10. First 90 days and first year

**First 90 days:** appoint an experienced semiconductor operations adviser; interview at least 20 potential customers; map existing Canadian services; select two plausible paid offerings; request tool, facility and outsourced-processing quotes; screen sites using documented utility requirements; identify the exact people and permits needed for the selected laboratory scope.

**Months 4–12:** complete paid work through partners where possible, settle a narrow service specification, obtain independent budget review and secure customer commitments before signing long equipment leases. Publish a capital-approval package with demand, price, capacity, uptime, gross contribution, staffing, maintenance, waste/utility needs, downside case and a closure/redeployment plan.

The 30-year ambition is a ladder of qualified capabilities. By 2056, local packaging, test and specialty-chip fabrication could be valuable achievements; globally competitive commodity DRAM remains a separate, contingent strategic choice.
