# Licensing and freedom to implement

## The intended permission

The project initiator wants others, including people with more experience in business development, to be able to use these plans freely. The repository's original documents, diagrams, data models, and code are made available under the [MIT License](../LICENSE). The license text includes associated documentation and permits commercial reuse subject to retaining its required notices. [Official MIT text](https://opensource.org/license/mit).

You may use the original material to form a business, change the business model, seek investment, publish a modified plan, build a product, or sell related services. You do not owe the project initiator a consulting fee, royalty, equity stake, or approval right merely because you used these plans. That is the project's intended permission; preserve the MIT notice when copying substantial original material.

Contributing improvements back is welcome and voluntary. The license does not require a business to release its private improvements or manufacturing know-how. It also cannot require an adopter to locate in Newfoundland. The geographical mission is an invitation and project objective, not an extra restriction added to MIT.

## What this does not grant

The repository can license only material contributors have the right to provide. Its license does not grant third-party patents, confidential process recipes, commercial EDA access, PDK rights, mask rights, semiconductor topography rights, trademarks, mineral rights, land rights, utility capacity, or permits. MIT's text contains no express third-party patent clearance. Each implementer must obtain rights applicable to its chosen design, process, product, and jurisdiction.

External source pages and documents remain under their own terms. Citation is not relicensing. Do not copy proprietary manuals, paid standards or NDA-controlled files into this repository. If future hardware or semiconductor design files are added, record their provenance and exact licenses; a separately licensed design remains under that license unless its rights holders authorize otherwise.

The four company names are working names. They do not establish incorporation, business-name availability, trademark clearance, or endorsement. The copyright holder label “Newfoundland Computers contributors” identifies contributors collectively for the notice; it does not assert that a corporation by that name exists.

## Why MIT was retained

MIT matches the requested permissive plan and avoids adding a share-back requirement. It is not an industrial patent license or a certification of the designs. If contributors later want a patent grant or a dedicated hardware license for a new design, consider that explicitly for the relevant files. Do not silently change the license of existing contributions or impose extra conditions while continuing to label them MIT.

## Implementation rights register

Before moving from a public plan to a specific commercial implementation, record:

| Item | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- |
| Original contributions | Author/provenance, license and any notices |
| CPU core, memory macro and other IP | License, commercial scope, modification and redistribution rights |
| EDA and PDK | Authorized users, supported release, permitted uses, publication restrictions |
| Process transfer | Rights to operate, alter, maintain, reproduce, export and support the process |
| Names and marks | Search/clearance decision and any required registration |
| Geological samples and land | Sampling permission, mineral tenure and land access |
| Customer work | IP ownership, disclosure permission, acceptance and liability terms |

This is a project policy for organizing evidence, not a claim that any required clearance has been completed.
