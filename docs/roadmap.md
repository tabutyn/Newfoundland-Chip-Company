# Roadmap and investment gates, 2026–2056

**Purpose:** Preserve the ambition of local RAM and computer production while making each major investment conditional on evidence. Dates are planning windows. Technical progress, customer qualification, and financing can move a window or stop a branch entirely.

## Roadmap

| Window | Work to pursue | Observable result | What this does not yet prove |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026–2028 | Open planning; customer discovery; recruit experienced technical/commercial leaders; verify material and facility claims | Published evidence, paid pilot proposals, independently reviewed initial scopes | Economic mine, profitable startups, or production-ready fab |
| 2028–2031 | Launch selected businesses; engineering and equipment-service exports; first externally fabricated designs; test/module services | Repeat customers, qualified deliverables, measured operating costs, traceable lots | Local chip fabrication or competitive memory production |
| 2031–2036 | Scale a validated tool or wafer niche; pilot fabrication where justified; CPU/control product and memory design competence | Customer-accepted products, reproducible process results, trained operators | Need for a billion-dollar factory |
| 2036–2046 | Evaluate specialty-fab, crystal-growth, or additional packaging investment | Transferable process, offtake commitments, utility agreements, financed construction and qualification | Advanced DRAM economics or independence from imports |
| 2046–2056 | Operate and renew viable capacity; consider dedicated memory partnership; broaden computer products | Saleable local semiconductor output, sustained employment and external revenue | That a fixed 2026 technology remains competitive in 2056 |

In every window, compare expansion against buying externally. Mature processes may remain useful for particular products, but demand must be demonstrated at the time of investment.

## Gates

These thresholds are proposed governance rules, not industry facts. Teams may change them through a recorded review, with a reason and replacement measure. Company-specific gates take precedence when they require stronger evidence than these shared minimums.

### G0 — Enough evidence to fund a startup

Required: a named accountable founder; an experienced domain reviewer; ten relevant customer interviews for that business; a chosen first product/service; documented purchase criteria; at least two credible paid-pilot prospects; a costed 18–24 month work plan; and a register of unresolved rights, access, and safety requirements.

**Decision:** Fund a bounded startup, continue inexpensive discovery, or stop. A polite expression of interest is not a customer order. If no customer will pay a viable price, do not finance equipment just to create momentum.

### G1 — A repeatable early business

Required: paid delivery to at least two unrelated customers, or a defensible explanation of one anchor customer's concentration risk; acceptance records; positive contribution margin on the chosen offering; a cash collection record; and service/warranty obligations included in the model.

**Decision:** Hire and expand only to serve a validated pipeline. Retain enough runway for a failed sale or a delayed customer qualification. This gate can pass before company-wide profitability but must show a route to it.

### G2 — A qualified manufacturing or tool pilot

Required: written customer specifications; licensed process/IP where needed; qualified incoming materials; repeatable results across several independently reviewed lots or builds; independent metrology; traceability; measured yield; a maintainable equipment plan; and trained staff. Customers set numerical acceptance thresholds.

**Decision:** Continue the pilot, redesign it, or close it. A best sample or one working die does not pass.

### G3 — Commit to a commercial plant

Required: independent pre-feasibility and engineering studies; a clearly defined product/capacity/process; executable technology-transfer plan; credible offtake or capacity-reservation contracts; site rights; utility capacity and quality commitments; water/waste/chemical logistics; approvals required for the authorized construction scope, with remaining permits on a funded schedule, and community engagement; major vendor quotations; construction and commissioning schedule; funded yield ramp; and independent review of downside cash requirements.

Finance must include operating losses during ramp, spare parts, requalification, inventory, customer receivables, insurance, and eventual closure obligations. Equipment sticker prices do not equal a complete project budget.

**Decision:** Commit capital only when the downside case is fundable. Otherwise maintain the smaller businesses and external manufacturing route.

### G4 — Operate qualified production

Required: repeated customer acceptance, measured process capability and reliability, saleable yield, delivery performance, support coverage, safe operating systems, and adequate working capital. Compare actual customer demand against capacity before an expansion.

**Decision:** Optimize, add capacity, change the product mix, or exit a product line. Record actual performance against the original investment thesis.

### G5 — Enter dedicated memory fabrication

Required: a specific memory architecture and target market; experienced process and product partner; necessary IP and transfer rights; independently validated cost-per-good-bit or cost-per-good-device; credible multi-year customers; financeable utilities and site; and capital sufficient for multiple process generations, qualification, and downturns.

**Decision:** Evaluate a dedicated memory program on its own merits. A successful CPU, SRAM prototype, or module assembly business does not pass this gate by itself. The advanced DRAM scenario remains outside the base case.

## Stop or redirect branches early

| Failed premise | Constructive next decision |
| --- | --- |
| Grey River material fails specification, cost, tenure, or environmental tests | Use a qualified external feedstock; investigate a different material business only with evidence |
| Equipment service cannot reach enough customers economically | Narrow the service territory/product family or partner with a mainland service network |
| Custom ASIC has insufficient volume | Keep the product on FPGA or a standard microcontroller and sell the system/service |
| Packaging or fab demand cannot support utilization | Continue outsourced production and local design/test rather than build idle capacity |
| Process-transfer or patent rights cannot be obtained | Select an accessible process/product or stop that branch |
| Power/water infrastructure cannot be secured at a viable site | Compare a different site or a less intensive capability |

## Review rhythm

Proposed operating practice: a quarterly evidence review, annual budget and source refresh, and an independent review before G2, G3, or G5. This document schedules no automated work and implies no existing review board. Appoint owners and obtain their agreement before relying on them.

Every annual review should answer: what customers paid, what was learned, what changed, what was stopped, what cash was spent, and what next decision that spending enables.
