# First 90 days: turn the proposal into evidence

**Status:** A proposed work programme, 21 October 2026. The clock begins when someone accepts responsibility for coordinating it. All owners below are **unassigned**. No interviews, outreach, sampling, facility bookings or financing applications have been performed by preparing this repository.

The objective is to select the next affordable experiment for each company and recruit people able to lead it. Success can mean advancing one company while deferring the others. The target is **40 relevant customer interviews: ten per company**, with records of purchase criteria and competing solutions. This is a proposed discovery target, not evidence of existing demand.

## Owners to recruit

| Workstream | Initial owner | Responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Programme coordination and evidence | Unassigned | Maintain backlog, source records, permissions, decisions and spending |
| Wafer commercial/technical lead | Unassigned | Wafer customers, supplier qualification and Grey River screening |
| Nanomachines commercial/technical lead | Unassigned | Service demand, narrow equipment scope and support obligations |
| Chip commercial/technical lead | Unassigned | Design customers, product requirements and manufacturing access |
| Foundry commercial/technical lead | Unassigned | Test/assembly demand, facility requirements and external production routes |
| Independent reviewers | Unassigned | Challenge technical, financial, geological and local assumptions |

One person may initially coordinate several workstreams. Before a startup passes G0, identify an accountable founder and an experienced reviewer for that domain. Seek business-development experience in industrial sales, qualification cycles and financing, alongside the appropriate engineering expertise. State time commitments, compensation expectations, conflicts of interest and authority explicitly.

## Days 0–30: define what to learn

Recruit the coordinator and prospective company leads. Ask each to write a one-page first-offering hypothesis: buyer, problem, competing solution, measurable benefit, specification, likely procurement route and reason a Newfoundland operation could deliver it.

Prepare an interview list with at least ten relevant prospective buyers per company, plus alternatives if people decline. Include customers outside Newfoundland. Ask about recent purchases, rejection criteria, procurement timing, service needs and acceptable commercial terms; avoid treating enthusiasm for regional development as purchase intent.

Create a source register separating verified facts, assumptions and hypotheses. Commission or scope a desk review of Grey River geology and rights, a current facility-access inventory, and a utility/site question list. Fieldwork and equipment commitments come only after appropriate permission and a defined test.

**Outputs by day 30:** four offering hypotheses; owner/adviser roster showing vacancies; interview plan; evidence/permission register; initial risk list; and a bounded budget for the next 60 days.

## Days 31–60: collect customer and technical evidence

Conduct the planned interviews as responsible owners arrange them. Use the same core questions, then add company-specific qualification questions. Record the buyer's actual current alternative and whether a paid pilot would be considered; document disagreement and refusals as useful evidence.

| Company | Evidence to seek during discovery |
| --- | --- |
| Wafer | Buyer-approved substrate specifications; supplier/laboratory budget quotations; traceability and return requirements; geological-review scope |
| Nanomachines | Equipment installed base and service pain points; acceptable response time; initial product limits; parts and warranty costs |
| Chip | A defined design problem; FPGA/standard-chip alternative; verification needs; available process/IP rights; packaging and test costs |
| Foundry | Paying demand for test, metrology or assembly; required measurements; partner quotes; realistic utilization and staffing |

Compare at least two plausible routes to each offering, such as owning a tool versus using a qualified partner. Prepare site scorecards only for the scope under consideration. Investigate [CMC's published prototyping services](https://www.cmc.ca/fab/) and the candidate routes in [local feasibility](local-feasibility.md); obtain current terms before including any access or support in the plan.

**Outputs by day 60:** interview summaries; emerging specification sheets; dated budget quotations where obtainable; comparison of delivery alternatives; and unresolved questions ranked by how much the answer changes the decision.

## Days 61–90: make four explicit decisions

Complete the 40-interview target or state the shortfall and its cause. Each company lead should propose one bounded experiment, its cost ceiling, acceptance measure and stopping rule. Identify at least two credible paid-pilot prospects for a business seeking to pass G0; an unsigned prospect remains distinct from an order.

Update the cash model using quotations, staffing, operating burn, inventory/receivables, contingency and qualification delays. Have an independent reviewer challenge customer concentration, technical dependencies, IP/access rights and the downside case. Write **advance, investigate further, defer, or stop** for each company, with reasons.

**Outputs by day 90:** four decision records; revised company plans and budget assumptions; next experiments with owners and deadlines; a public evidence summary; and a list of confidential evidence retained outside the public repository. If G0 is not met, continue inexpensive discovery instead of announcing a factory.

## Optional shared discovery financing

Volunteers can begin research and interviews without raising a large fund. For a professionally staffed **6–12-month shared feasibility programme**, the following is an original, low-confidence **C$250,000–750,000** planning envelope in constant 2026 CAD. It is separate from the four startup envelopes and is not an additional 90-day spending requirement.

| Use | Low | High |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Commercial and technical leads | C$100,000 | C$250,000 |
| Specialist assays and reviews | C$50,000 | C$200,000 |
| Travel and customer discovery | C$25,000 | C$75,000 |
| Feasibility, administration and IP review | C$25,000 | C$100,000 |
| Contingency | C$50,000 | C$125,000 |
| **Total** | **C$250,000** | **C$750,000** |

This scope excludes land purchases, construction, major machinery, mine development and full plant engineering. Release spending against useful deliverables. If a funded startup absorbs the same work, remove the overlap from the combined budget. **Assume zero public support and zero speculative sales receipts when calculating the financing need**; revise only for documented agreements and realistic cash timing.

## Publish evidence responsibly

For each source or interview record, retain date, method, relevant scope, permission status and the claim it supports. Publish original analysis, public-source links and permission-cleared summaries. Do not upload confidential quotations, personal contacts, proprietary process data or whole third-party reports under the MIT licence. Record negative results and changed assumptions as carefully as successes.

At the end of the programme, readers should know what was learned, what remains uncertain, who owns the next task, what it costs, and what result would justify further investment. Use the [roadmap and gates](roadmap.md) to keep the 2056 ambition connected to those concrete decisions.

## Company-specific depth

The shared 40-interview target is a minimum initial screen. Where a company plan requires more interviews, paid customers or qualification evidence, that stricter gate applies before that company advances. The Chip Company, for example, proposes at least 20 potential-customer interviews. Do not count the same interview twice in a combined program merely because it informs two companies.
