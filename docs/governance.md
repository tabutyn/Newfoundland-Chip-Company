# Governance and handoff to implementers

## A repository is not a holding company

This project publishes and improves plans. It does not incorporate the four proposed businesses, allocate their shares, appoint directors, bind investors, or commit public funds. Each implementation must establish its own legal ownership, management, contracts, financing and operating accountability.

A maintainer can accept documentation changes without approving an industrial investment. An investor or operator must evaluate a project independently of GitHub popularity. An implementer may fork the plans under MIT and does not need a central organization to proceed.

## Proposed roles

All roles below are unassigned until a person explicitly accepts them.

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Repository steward | Keep the mission, links, releases and contribution process coherent |
| Evidence editor | Check claim/source matching, dates, scope and uncertainty labels |
| Four company-plan leads | Maintain customer hypotheses, unit economics, work packages and next decisions |
| Independent domain reviewers | Review mineral, process, equipment, IC-design or fab claims within their expertise |
| Finance reviewer | Reconcile capital, operating cash, funding sources and downside sensitivity |
| Community/workforce liaison | Organize an appropriate engagement and skills plan with consent |

Do not list people, institutions or companies as advisers or partners without their agreement. Affiliation does not establish permission to speak for an institution.

## Decision records

Use the [decision template](../templates/decision-record.md) for a major product choice, revised budget, proposed plant, process selection or resource decision. Include alternatives, evidence, uncertainties, what would reverse the decision, owner, and next review trigger.

Evidence states are: **unverified**, **supported for planning**, **externally reviewed**, and **customer/contract qualified**. They describe specific claims, not the maturity of the entire repository. A merged pull request does not promote a claim automatically.

## Reviews and releases

Prefer small contributions with explicit source links. A budget or capability change should update the affected company plan, financial assumptions and top-level summary in one pull request. An independent second review is proposed before publishing a new claim of resource suitability, production yield, customer qualification or construction readiness.

Use tagged releases for coherent snapshots. Keep historical budgets and decisions in Git history; correct errors plainly in the next version. Proposed quarterly reviews and annual source refreshes are responsibilities for future maintainers, not actions already scheduled or performed.

## Commercial and community integrity

Contributors should disclose material commercial interests in recommendations. Compare related-party transactions against external alternatives. Record intercompany revenue separately from revenue earned from customers outside the group.

Early public participation should be meaningful: identify affected communities and rights holders before selecting a disruptive site or extraction plan, explain options and uncertainties, and document unresolved concerns accurately. The project should also plan paid training, safe working conditions, worker voice and transparent employment practices. These are proposed values and work requirements, not evidence of existing programs.

## Useful handoff package for a future founder

A plan is ready for serious founder review when it contains a defined customer problem, chosen first product, competitive alternatives, evidence of willingness to pay, an experienced delivery lead, clear technical acceptance criteria, a bottom-up 24-month cash plan, a rights/access register, and a bounded first investment decision. That package is the next objective of version 0.1.
