# Newfoundland feasibility: evidence before a site decision

**Status:** Proposed due diligence, 21 October 2026. All locations, resource rights, utility allocations, facility access, partnerships and funding remain unconfirmed. No outreach has been performed through this planning exercise. Owners must be recruited before the work below is commissioned.

## 1. Test the Grey River premise separately

The provincial Grey River tungsten project sheet describes quartz-bearing mineralization; it does not establish semiconductor feedstock, an economic silica reserve, or rights available to this project. Treat the location as a candidate for investigation. [Government project sheet](https://www.gov.nl.ca/em/files/mines-investments-companysheets-tungsten.pdf)

Commission a qualified geologist to identify the exact target, review historical records and current mineral tenure, establish land/access permissions, and design representative sampling. An independent laboratory and process specialist should evaluate mineralogy, impurity distribution, sample variability, processing recovery and buyer-specific limits. Record detection limits, sample chain of custody and rejected samples. Compare the resulting delivered product with purchased feedstock, including waste, transport, energy and closure costs.

Keep qualification for silicon-smelter feedstock separate from qualification for high-purity crucible quartz. Neither establishes electronic-grade polysilicon or finished wafer capability. Stop the Grey River branch if its rights, material quality, economics or environmental case fail; the other businesses can still use qualified external suppliers.

## 2. Match each operation to an appropriate site

A design office, equipment workshop, wafer-finishing line, silicon refinery and fab have different needs. Do not presume a fab belongs near a mine. Compare at least two credible locations for each physical investment scope, including a partner-hosted option where feasible.

NL Hydro describes growing Island demand and a need for additional capacity and energy. Consequently, this plan assumes **neither spare industrial capacity nor cheap firm electricity**. Obtain a project-specific connection study, tariff estimate, service date and reliability assessment. [NL Hydro planning questions](https://nlhydro.com/power-the-province/your-questions-answered/)

For electricity, quantify peak demand, annual energy, power-quality tolerance, interruption consequences, restoration requirements and backup scope. Assess shared failure modes before treating two feeds as redundant. For water, quantify source quality, seasonal availability, peak and average demand, ultrapure-water treatment, reject streams, reuse, effluent limits and treatment capacity. A municipal water connection is not evidence of a qualified process-water system.

### Proposed site scorecard

These weights are proposed for a future process facility; record justified changes for other business types. Each cell must cite a dated study, quotation or responsible authority. Mark missing evidence **U**, not an optimistic numerical score.

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence to obtain |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Power capacity, quality and reliability | 20% | Utility study; delivered tariff; connection scope/date; interruption and backup analysis |
| Water and effluent | 15% | Source tests; seasonal balance; treatment design; discharge pathway; capital/operating estimate |
| Skilled workforce and training | 15% | Role/shift plan; recruitment evidence; paid training pathway; maintenance coverage |
| Logistics and service response | 10% | Equipment route survey; freight quotations; gases/chemicals supply; critical-spares and service plan |
| Land, building and expansion | 10% | Verified rights; geotechnical review; building suitability; expansion and installation costs |
| Community and Indigenous engagement | 10% | Identified affected parties; agreed engagement approach; concerns and unresolved issues |
| Commercial and technical support | 10% | Customer access; laboratories; supplier support; qualified contractors; data connectivity |
| Physical and climate resilience | 5% | Site-specific flood, wind, corrosion and access assessment; mitigation costs |
| Housing and worker services | 5% | Housing availability/cost study; transport and family-service needs; recruitment implications |
| **Total** | **100%** | |

Use 0 for a demonstrated failure, 1 for major unresolved obstacles, 3 for a supported workable plan, and 5 for strong verified evidence; document intermediate ratings. Calculate `sum(weight × score / 5)` only when all criteria have evidence. Report uncertainties beside the result. Unresolved land rights, essential utility feasibility or required authorizations are hard stops and cannot be offset by a high weighted score.

## 3. Establish a workforce and facility inventory

Memorial's Chemistry facilities page lists micro/nanofabrication equipment and describes a cleanroom as under construction. Treat that description as an inventory lead requiring current confirmation, not proof of available production capacity. Verify tool condition, materials compatibility, staff support, external-user eligibility, rates, scheduling, IP terms and permitted work. [Memorial facilities description](https://www.mun.ca/chem/future-students/future-graduate-students/major-facilities/)

Prepare a skills matrix for design/verification, vacuum systems, metrology, process integration, facilities, industrial maintenance, quality and shift operations. Ask training providers to assess paid placements and curricula against actual job tasks. Recruit experienced operators to supervise the first processes; a list of university equipment or graduates does not establish a production team. Include housing and retention costs in recruitment planning.

## 4. Plan engagement and approvals early

Identify potentially affected communities, Indigenous governments and organizations, landholders and resource users with appropriate advice. Invite participation while alternatives remain open; document concerns, proposed benefits and unresolved issues without claiming endorsement. Establish the applicable consultation and approval responsibilities for the actual site and activity. The province publishes a land/resource consultation policy and an environmental-assessment guide; neither a repository discussion nor a private meeting substitutes for required processes. [Consultation policy](https://www.gov.nl.ca/exec/irr/overview/aboriginal-consultation-policy/) · [Environmental-assessment guide](https://www.gov.nl.ca/eccc/files/GUIDE-TO-THE-PROCESS_Aug-2025.pdf)

## Required decision package

Publish a dated, permission-cleared summary of resource findings, site comparisons, utility scope, workforce gaps, engagement status, cost uncertainty and reasons to proceed or stop. Retain confidential quotations and personal records separately. Use the [roadmap gates](roadmap.md); a promising locality alone does not pass the plant-investment gate.
